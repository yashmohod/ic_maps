# Feature comparison

Campus-map features checked against Google Maps, Apple Maps, and the Uber rider app. **5** means add it soon. **1** means leave it out. A feature that only exists in the editor, or only halfway, is listed as missing.

## Features we have

- **Place search.** Type a campus building or lot and pick a match.
- **Saved places.** Favorite buildings, shown on the map and in settings.
- **Standard map and satellite.** Switch between them in the side menu.
- **Your location.** A dot and an accuracy ring.
- **Heading.** The map turns with you during live navigation.
- **Dark mode.**
- **Walking and driving directions.** Pedestrian or vehicular, one route at a time.
- **Multi-stop trips.**
- **Distance and ETA.** Miles or kilometers.
- **Live navigation.** The map follows you, with a tilted camera.
- **Next-turn banner and the full step list.** Outdoor turns and indoor steps.
- **Automatic reroute.** A new path after you stay about 25 meters off the line for 2.5 seconds.
- **Avoid stairs, limit steep slopes, and route through buildings.**
- **Park, then walk.** Recommended lots, and a choice when a building has more than one.
- **Send a route.** A shareable link and a QR code.
- **Draw and share your own map.** My Maps, including collaborators.
- **Report a problem.** Map, route, and accessibility reports, with a photo.

## Features we do not have

| Urgency | Feature | Why it is or is not worth adding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | Show building hours on the place card | Hours are already stored in the destination editor. Visitors never see them. |
| 5 | A recenter button | The map finds you on open and follows you during live navigation. If you pan away, there is no button to jump back. |
| 5 | Spoken directions | The steps are already written. Reading them aloud matters while walking, and it matches the accessibility reports already collected. |
| 4 | A public blue-light layer | Editors can mark blue-light phones. Visitors cannot see them. |
| 4 | A step-free route | Avoid stairs and the incline limit exist. There is no single wheelchair choice, and elevators are not guaranteed. |
| 4 | Remember where you parked | A drive ends at a lot. Nothing saves that spot so you can walk back to the car. |
| 4 | Start from somewhere other than your GPS dot | Useful for planning a trip from a building you are not standing at. An Uber-style draggable pickup pin is not the point. |
| 4 | Recent searches | The same buildings get looked up repeatedly. |
| 4 | Walk and drive times side by side | Both modes exist. You have to switch and request again to compare them. |
| 3 | Browse a floor plan while visiting | Floor plans are in the editor, and a trip can route inside. A visitor cannot open the floors on their own. |
| 3 | A dorm or usual-place shortcut | Favorites cover this in a general way. A pinned dorm is a smaller version of Home and Work. |
| 3 | More than one route to the same place | The app returns one path. On a campus that is often enough. |
| 3 | Share your live location | Useful for safety. It is not required to follow a route. |
| 3 | Offline maps | Worth it for basements and dead spots. The campus is small and usually connected, so it can wait. |
| 2 | Several saved lists or a public guide | One favorites row and favorite trips are enough for now. |
| 2 | Let visitors edit the map | Reports are the safer public tool. Open editing of the graph can wait. |
| 2 | Search by category | Only worth it if the map grows past buildings and lots. |
| 2 | A nearby browse | Same limit: the map is a building list, not a city directory. |
| 2 | Cycling directions | A real campus use, and a new graph mode. |
| 2 | Bus or shuttle directions | Useful once the app is meant to leave campus. Not part of the walking and driving graph. |
| 2 | Plan a future leave or arrive time | Class schedules would make this matter. The router does not use time of day today. |
| 2 | 3D buildings | Live navigation already tilts the camera. Modeled buildings are polish. |
| 1 | Reviews, ratings, busy times, and place photos | A directory product, not this one. |
| 1 | Street View or Look Around | The satellite photos are overhead. Street imagery is a different collection. |
| 1 | Traffic, terrain, transit lines, bike paths, air quality, wildfire | City-map layers. They do not help a campus path graph. |
| 1 | Lane guidance and speed cameras | Road-driving features for a city, not campus paths. |
| 1 | Augmented-reality walking | The camera follow and spoken steps come first. |
| 1 | Nearby cars, ride types, prices, and seats | That is a ride-hailing product. |
| 1 | Request a driver, then show the driver, car, and plate | Vehicular mode means the visitor is driving. |
| 1 | A live assigned car, payment, and calling the driver | Same. |
| 1 | Ride receipts and a commercial trip history | Favorite trips and shareable routes already save the trips worth keeping. |
